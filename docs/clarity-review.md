## 1. Verdict

The owner is right about s2e01. He is largely right about the dialogue in all 17 episodes. He is half right about the larger plot.

- **What all five cold viewers agree on:** every episode has 3 to 7 lines a first-time viewer cannot decode. The spine of both series still comes through by the end. They scored S1 5.5-6, S2 5.5-6.5, and s2e01 alone 4-5.
- **The plots are not broken.** They are told backwards: names, places, motives and rules arrive after the scenes that depend on them, so the viewer only understands in hindsight.
- **Neither premise is too complex for 5-6 two-minute episodes.** S2 is close to the limit, and its mechanics overload it, not its premise.
- **The causes are systemic.** They come from how the gates were built, not from one writer, so the fix has to include a permanent gate.

## 2. Ranked causes (most damaging first)

**1. The facts live in the bible, and every gate checked against the bible.**
- The script-critique skill hands the critic "the script, the bible and the specification". Its 8-criterion rubric has no clarity score; CLARITY is only a label, used at medium.
- Every critic verdict I read scored 7.7-8.3, and all 17 scripts passed Gate 2. Cold viewers scored the same episodes 4-7.5.
- Example: the s2e01 critic noted "'shut cinema' from a still" as medium and passed the episode at 7.7. Two viewers scored that episode 4-5.

**2. The length measure squeezed out the connecting lines.**
- The 1,050-character measure (D-005 change 9, D-006 change 8, D-007 direction 1, D-016 direction 7) was set "before any speech was measured" (D-020).
- Cuts now run 100-110 s against a 150 s ceiling, so about 30-45 s per episode is unused (estimate).
- What was cut was set-up. s2e01 b6 is staged "Cut in mid-report: she has just told him", so Tripathi's "Arre, talkies?" answers a line nobody hears. D-006 change 2 paid for a balance line by cutting "the carbon-copy sentence and the eleven copies" from s2e03 b3. D-007 direction 2 cut the s1e02 drill to two lines.

**3. The rules removed every cheap way to orient the viewer.**
- The rubric fails a hook that "opens on set-up".
- The S2 pitch hook was "Opens mid-scene, no explanation" (loglines.json).
- The plan says "No narrator unless the story needs one".
- D-004 direction 6 says "Nothing the plot needs may depend on reading" an overlay.
- Together these left no tool for saying who someone is, where we are, or what a word means.

**4. Clues that carry the plot are pictures only, in a stills format at phone size.**

- S1: the fresh-milk swap in s1b02, which is never said.
- S2: the cap as the tell that he is fake ("both wear the same uniform and the same lantern patch").
- S2: the cinema reveal in s2e01 b3, with blank posters.

## 3. (a) Plot level

**Is the premise too complex?**
- S1: no. One room, two people, one secret, one coded call. A viewer has to hold about 12 facts by the choice.
- S2: borderline. A viewer has to hold about 16 facts and three senior men by the choice, and two stations share one name. Its spine is simple: "the fake station takes the complaints the real one buries; whom do you arrest?" The load comes from mechanics: slip and stamp, who knows Nirala is fake, envelopes, charge handover, the transfer.
- The "one night" design in branch-map.md makes it worse: "Tripathi phones Bhaiyaji and tells him the Inspector is a fake." The viewer is meant to vote not knowing this. The only plant, "So he never told the department. Or Bhaiyaji.", reads as a contradiction, because Bhaiyaji walks in and says "Our outpost, our Inspector sahab."

**Central question.**
- S2: lands at the end of s2e01 ("who do I arrest?"), but only for viewers who worked out that the peanut man is Tripathi.
- S1: viewers read it as "will he get his revenge?" The real spine, who Kiran is, is hidden so well that viewer 3 said it "doesn't feel like a mystery until it is answered."

**Stakes.**
- S1: Aditya's threat is the clearest line in either series ("I know how much puts you to sleep, and how much means you never wake up"). Kiran's own stake ("Mausi has eighteen lakh of mine") is said once in s1e01 and is missing at the choice.
- S2: the risk in A lands ("Alive", "He has a real one", "named in the same FIR"). The reward in B is stated only after the vote, in s2b01 ("The slip, the sack count, and Bhaiyaji's own voice"). At the vote, "only he can get inside" has no object.

**Who wants what.**
- S1: Aditya states two goals back to back ("the woman who sends these brides" / "I want this hand").
- S2: the DSP wants an arrest before "press conference at ten tomorrow". What the conference is for (a zero-crime shield) is first said in s2a03.

**The choice.**
- S1: understood, but it costs Kiran nothing that is named, and "He drank it" has no stated consequence for a man who is awake. The ballot meaning uses a name never spoken.
- S2: understood as obey or defy, but at the vote the reward is not clear. "I can't stop a truck with his stamp" muddles who stamps what.

**Antagonist and twist.**
- S1: "the hand is hers" rests on a 1-2 s silent insert. Kiran's turn in B has no line about Mohit's death.
- S2: Bhaiyaji is a name with no face in s2e01. Tripathi is named 60 s after his face. "So the invitation was never for the stamp. It was for you." is right, but depends on a fact the viewer never heard.

**Branches and endings.** These pay off; the finales are the clearest episodes (viewers scored them 7-7.5).
- What lands: "Tulsi", "Pour in the whole sachet, Chhoti", "Next! Token one.", "the bench is full", "Item six".
- Open on S1: the vow (both branches), "Only saffron in the milk" (read as a contradiction), and the police arriving in s1a03 after a line nobody reacted to.
- Open on S2 branch B: the code phrase spoken after "the line has gone dead", the missing Item two, and the transfer and press conference never resolved.
- Open on S2 branch A: the DSP's turn has no motive.

**What the bible intended against what reaches the screen:**
- s1e01 b5: hearing "Kiran" should tell the viewer that Sneha is false. No viewer linked the names; Mausi says it off screen, in the lane.
- s1e01 b9: a full-frame photograph with the crescent scar. Missed or too small; no line says what it shows.
- s1e02 b9: Mausi drills Billu out of Aditya's hearing. Billu has no line and is not named, so viewers could not tell who she was talking to, or when. No frames exist yet.
- s2e01 b3: the wide shot reveals a cinema. Viewers saw "an ordinary police station".
- s2e01 b5: the railway geography. It comes after "this side of the line" (b4).
- s2e01 b6: "She reports what she saw." The report was cut.
- s2e01 b8: the DSP gives "the reason for the hour". The reason is never said.
- D-004: Nirala's uniform reads as fake in silhouette. Viewers called the uniforms identical.

## 4. (b) Dialogue level, ranked

1. **Missing set-up and replies to unheard lines** (see cause 7). Worst in s2e01 b1 to b6 and s1e02 b3.
2. **Names not introduced** (cause 5). Fix: every speaking character is named or given a role on screen at first appearance.
3. **Code words and jargon** (cause 6). Each term needs one gloss in context, in English especially, for example "the malkhana, the evidence room". Hindi needs fewer glosses.
4. **Compression to hit the length measure.** Missing "because" and "so" clauses, as in "Take it all. Open the padlock." and "Mohit.". There is now room to restore them.
5. **Missing connectives and pronouns with nothing to refer to**, as in "Keep them safe" and the s2b01 "it" and "this".
6. **Code phrases:**
   - S1's "He drank it" / "The milk has split" works.
   - S2's "The tea has gone cold" is spoken after the line is dead.
   - S1's "No code" lie is undercut because the drill that exposes it has no clear listener.
7. **Too many numbers in S2:** keep three load-bearing numbers an episode at most.
8. **The no-narrator rule:** keep it. Allow orientation supers instead (name and role at first appearance; place and time). These need an Arbiter ruling against D-004 direction 6.
9. **Critics scored craft over clarity** (cause 1).

The Hindi is no better structured than the English. This is a writing problem, not a translation problem.

## 5. Options (costs are estimates)

Assumptions behind the costs:
- Voice: about 1 credit a character. One full re-voice of an episode in both languages is about 2,150-2,750 characters.
- Credits used so far: about 66,700 of about 730,000.
- Frames: about 2 minutes each, on 2-3 Codex slots.
- Only s1e01 (28 frames) and s2e01 (30 made plus 6 owed placeholders) have frames. The other 15 episodes are at storyboard, paused, so restructuring them costs storyboards, not frames.

### Option 1. Dialogue-only clarity pass on all 17 (beats and frames kept)

**What changes:**
- About 40% of lines are reworded.
- About 250 Hindi and 300 English characters are added per episode, using the spare length.
- Names, glosses and connecting clauses go in; numbers come out.
- The vow, Kiran's motive and Nirala's stamping get one line each.
- The choice states what each answer costs and gains.
- The ballot meanings are synced (the app's series.json still carries old text).
- Edit-only fixes: phone and off-screen voices marked in subtitles, and the s31 photograph held at least 2.5 s with a push-in on the scar (no new frame).

**What is kept:** every beat, every frame, the story and both endings.

**Cost:**
- Voice: about 25,000-35,000 credits.
- Frames: about 0-6 (only the s2e01 cards already owed), so 0-12 minutes. s1e01 and s2e01 need a re-edit.
- Time: about 5-7 hours.

**Risk:** low.
- The extra lines may turn into exposition ("as you know") or push cuts toward 150 s.
- It cannot fix the order problems in s2e01: the railway shown after the line that names it, the cinema reveal, and why she is there.

### Option 2. Restructure within the same premise and cast

**What changes:** everything in Option 1, plus:
- New or reordered beats where order is the fault.
- Orientation supers for names, roles, places and times.
- S2 mechanics pruned:
  - one disambiguating name for each station ("the real thana" / "the talkies thana");
  - the transfer thread either cut or paid off in both branches;
  - Bhaiyaji's belief that Nirala is a real, bought officer stated outright;
  - a way through the stamp and slip logic that a viewer can follow.

**What is kept:** the premise, cast, plates, both branches, both endings, and the lines viewers liked.

**Cost:**
- Scoped as I recommend (S2 shared episodes restructured, Option 1 on the rest): about 30,000-38,000 credits, and about 14-18 frames for s2e01 including the 6 owed (30-40 minutes). The branch storyboards are redone with about 2-3 extra frames each (about 1-1.5 hours of frame time).
- Applied to all 17: about 48,000-55,000 credits and about 55-65 extra frames.
- Time: about 9-12 hours, including an Arbiter entry and a bible delta.

**Risk:** medium. New beats bring new continuity checks and one more review round.

### Option 3. A simpler premise

**S2 example from the same cast and plates:** "SI must jail the fake inspector by the 10 a.m. zero-crime press conference; his register proves her own SHO buries complaints; arrest him, or put his thirty complaints in the real register and name her SHO on stage." The ration truck, the godown and the court go.

**What it throws away:**
- Two approved bible gates and the choice-balance work.
- The beats viewers liked best: "It takes no chest to hear a complaint", the "Item six" memo, the court's yes-or-no trap, "Next! Token one".
- Most of the 30 frames in s2e01, and about 20,000 credits of voice already spent.

**Cost:**
- Voice: about 26,000-30,000 credits.
- Frames: about 25-30 regenerated.
- Time: about 14-20 hours before wave 2 can resume.

**Risk:** high. The story is new and untested, it may come out blander, and the owner liked the flow and direction.

S1 does not need a new premise.

## 7. A permanent clarity gate

**C1, script, before voice:**
- Two fresh sessions per series read each path cold, one in Hindi and one in English.
- They get no bible, loglines, notes or decisions. They get only the speakers, the spoken lines, one line per shot of what the camera shows (not what it means), and the ballot text.
- Each fills a fixed form:
  - the premise in one sentence after the first third of e01 and after each episode;
  - who is who;
  - what the lead wants and what she could lose;
  - both options at the choice, each with its cost and its gain;
  - the lines they could not decode, quoted;
  - a 0-10 score.
- Pass only if all of these hold:
  - 7 or more in both languages;
  - the premise is stated within the first third of e01;
  - every speaker is identified at first appearance;
  - both costs and both gains are named before the vote;
  - no more than 2 undecoded lines an episode, none of them carrying the plot;
  - no line answers something the viewer did not hear.
- Anything less is fatal in script-critique.

**C2, storyboard or animatic, before frames:**
- A cold viewer checks that every clue the plot rests on can be read at phone size, and that every line's speaker is on screen or marked.
- A plot clue that is a picture must also be spoken once.

**C3, render:** screening adds the same form.

**Changes to the skills and the measure** (an Arbiter entry is needed; I cannot edit skills):
- script-critique gets a 9th criterion, clarity, scored from the C1 report and not from the critic's own read.
- Each bible gets a viewer ledger: per episode, every fact the viewer must know and the line or readable image that carries it.
- hinglish-screenwriting gets five rules:
  - name or role at first appearance;
  - jargon glossed once in context;
  - no more than three load-bearing numbers an episode;
  - a planted thread pays off in both branches or is cut;
  - phone and off-screen voices are marked.
- The character measure is replaced by a 130-140 s target under the 150 s ceiling, and clarity lines are not cut to meet a count.
